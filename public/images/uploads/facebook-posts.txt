(PLEASE KNOW THIS CHILD SURVIVED)
14 seconds.
That's how long this child was in the water before anyone realized what had happened.
This image is a powerful reminder of how quickly and silently drowning can occur.
1- The children were supposed to be out of the pool.
2- One adult was actually in the pool.
3- One adult was tending to another child.
4- One adult was sitting just feet away.
YET NO ONE HEARD THE CHILD ENTER THE WATER!
Notice the flotation device left on the deck. Many children who regularly wear flotation devices have not yet developed the skills to save themselves if they enter the water without one.
Thankfully, this child survived. Tragically, many others do not.
We are not posting this to shame anyone. It's about understanding that drowning doesn't look or sound the way most people expect. It is often silent, and it can happen in seconds—even when adults are nearby.
That's why we stress layers of protection: self-rescue swim lessons, barriers, active adult supervision when children are in or near the water, and never relying on flotation devices.
It only takes as little as 21 seconds to lose a child to a drowning. PLEASE DO NOT LET YOUR CHILD BECOME A STATISTIC.

Around 800 children die from drowning every year in the U.S.—but the real number is likely much higher.
That’s the equivalent of 11 full school buses of children gone… every single year.
This graphic is shocking, but it doesn’t tell the full story.
 Drowning deaths are severely underreported.
If a child is pulled from the water alive but later dies at the hospital, the cause of death is often labeled as cardiac arrest or brain damage—not drowning. That means the statistics we rely on don’t fully reflect the real impact.
 And for every child who dies, eight more are treated in emergency rooms for nonfatal drownings.
These children may survive, but many suffer lifelong neurological damage—losing motor function, the ability to speak, or even basic brain activity. Families’ lives are forever changed.
At the Judah Brown Project, we fight to make this invisible epidemic visible. Because behind every number is a child who should still be here—and a family who now lives with unimaginable loss. See less

Now that we are inpatient and watching Frozen 2 on loop, I remembered that I wrote in the Fall of 2022, and it has been sitting unposted in my vault. This one applies more now than ever and was a great reminder that WE HAVE THIS!
***********************************************
The Next Right Thing… (written Fall of 2022)
Anyone else feel lost in the journey sometimes? Our life started to feel like a mix of flashback scenes from a Lifetime movie. Girl marries her high school sweetheart and has a boat load of adorable kiddos. In a single blink, everything goes haywire. Their baby drowns. They do CPR on his lifeless body.  He survives but his brain injury causes his body to twist, and bones contort. They search the country and find a doctor willing to operate before his spine crushes his organs.  The drama continues… Two years later, her father-in-law dies in her husband’s arms. She does CPR again, unsuccessfully.   Then everyone needs to wear mask and quarantine while a “virus” affects people all over the world. They lose more loved ones.  Their kids’ high school and college graduations are cancelled. Their jobs are nonexistent, so they search for the next right thing.  Then, a plot twist, he is teaching ISR swim lessons, and she is back in school, wearing blue scrubs, while reciting medical facts. End scene.    Anyone else confused?  Time to change the channel, right.  But who can?  I, for one, am invested in the “happily ever after.” 
There is a song in one of Adam’s favorite Disney movies that hits deep. Hard to imagine that animation could make such an impact, but this anthem has become a guide through the last couple years.
So, I'll walk through this night
Stumbling blindly toward the light
And do the next right thing
And with the dawn what comes then?
When it's clear that everything will never be the same again
Then I'll make the choice to hear that voice
And do the next right thing
Since the day Adam was released from the PICU in 2017, I have informally been one of his medical caregivers for the last 5 years. However, in the Fall of 2021, I walked across a stage and graduated as an official pediatric trach/vent nurse.
At the ceremony, my teacher quoted Mark Twain when she said, 
“The two most important days of your life is the day you were born and the day you understand why”.
The decision to become a nurse at 43 seemed a bit crazy to some but I knew deep inside, for me, this was “the next right thing.”
Nursing school wasn’t always something I wanted to pursue.  I’m a professional landman, novice writer of unpublished children’s books, research expert and self-proclaimed designer of all things pretty. My lane is creative, messy and unpredictable. Nursing is sterile and by the book. I like a good challenge.  I did not, however, consider how hard it would be to separate school from the past and the medical world we live in now.  
Experience is a double edge sword and an advantage I did not want.  Literally, chapter after chapter reflected concepts that I previously lived through with family members.  I began to hate that I could answer questions before choices were presented.  While many could not even pronounce medications, I knew the color, dosage, and side effects of at least 10 that Adam gets daily.  I was angry that my son had been my own muse for adventitious lung sounds. When I listened to ‘normal’ lungs, without thinking, I would check my stethoscope to make certain it was working because the crackles, wheezing and rhonchi were not there.  And don’t get me started on seizures… I’ve missed so many questions based on the actual protocol we use versus what is taught in books.  
Triggers were everywhere but slowly I understood that somehow, every moment, every step thus far, had been preparing me for one of my most challenging years yet.  To put 2020 and 2021 into perspective, nursing school was the easiest part of my day.  Life on the other hand was an ever-changing battle of events.  
Within the first months of enrollment, Adam’s night nurse quit without notice.  In addition to being a full-time student, I had to cover 90 hours of approved nursing that three agencies couldn’t staff. I was really struggling.   Rickey was in Florida for 2 months studying to be a survival swim instructor and the flu and Covid passed through our home 3 times. The more days I would spend watching Adam sick, the harder it was to take the next step, but then the song, and his smile, reminded me how far we’d all come. So day after day, I would throw on my scrubs, brave the snow, and walk two icy blocks in downtown Altoona before the sun would rise for hospital clinicals.
By the grace of God and with the help of a new night crew, my focus became clear again and we pushed through the 2020-2021 school year.  Adam had scoliosis surgery twice, his submandibular glands were removed, and he was treated for a broken femur.  Grace had a snowboarding accident, resulting in reconstruction surgery with a plate and 13 screws, a week of hospitalization and a missed semester in Hawaii.  With school closures, Lea struggled with isolation and needed to relearn to read.  Avery battled through her Senior year of event cancellations, sport quarantines and even Covid itself. 
Despite all these challenges, and amid a crazy, intensive year full of obstacles no one saw coming, our family started offering swim lessons and held multiple fundraisers for our nonprofit. 
We often hear “I don’t know how you do it?” 
The fact is, we’re not interested in the alternative.  We’ve been there. It involves lying on the floor in the fetal position and it’s not fun. We keep busy, so we don’t go backwards. We work hard to show our children that it’s okay to get knocked down, it’s okay to grieve, but it is our responsibility to quietly get up, keep pushing forward and do the next right thing.
In addition to our careers in energy, I now work on the weekends for Bayada as one of Adam’s nurses. My husband is an ISR instructor and has taught over 150* children to self-rescue. (*as of 2023) Our children are discovering their own ‘why’, and our family’s health is stable. 
As I walked across the stage August of 2021, proudly graduating with high honors and 4.0 GPA (yay me!) I saw my family cheering me on and realized “THEY” are, and always will be, “MY WHY”. I was born to be their teacher, cheerleader, voice of reason (Ric thinks this one’s debatable), role model, life coach, caregiver, bright light of positivity and, in the face of uncertainty, their biggest advocate. 
As important as those new shiny credentials are behind my name, they will never come close to the ones I already had: 
MA. MAMA. MOM. And even sometimes BRUH. 
**************************************************
A huge thank you to everyone who helped us along the way, especially our lovely nurses. If you have a picture with Adam, please post it!
Of course, our Lifetime movie is far from over, but I hope it can give comfort to those struggling. When the storyline doesn’t make sense, take a single step and try the next right thing. You may end up finding yourself in search of the why.
What's your Why?

My friend Christi, at the Judah Brown Project, advocates tirelessly for years against the use of Puddle Jumpers. She, and other families who have lost their child to drowning, receive so much backlash and ugly comments daily from others who think they are trying to deflect and blame anything but themselves for their child drowning.  This cannot be further from the truth. 
The truth is we are all human and when we hear story after story of a child who drowned, moments after taking off the device that kept them afloat, then it is our responsibility to admit that the VERY DEVICE we were using to protect them, is actually the REASON they feel so CONFIDENT jumping back in, with or without it.  
PLEASE ASK YOURSELF if YOUR CHILD would know what happens if they were to go back in without the device on? Moments later after eating a pool snack? Hours later during clean up? Days later when the family is inside but the door/gate isn't shut properly? Remember 69% of children drown in normal clothes, not swimsuits.
At the end of the day, water is beautiful and fun, until it's not. If a layer of protection fails (supervision, gates, alarms, etc), would your child KNOW that the device was keeping them afloat or would they jump in CAREFREE and drown?
Please watch the short video.  Enroll your child in survival swim lessons so they can RESPECT the water. Hold your non-swimmer and/or be within an arm's length in the pool.  If this seems like too much or takes the fun out of swimming, then buy or visit a splash pad.  
THERE ARE FAR WORSE THINGS YOU COULD BE REQUIRED TO DO IN A DAY. TRUST US!
- Shannon Matherne, mother of a drowning survivor and Water Safety Advocate

God, please wrap your arms around my hometown, our special friends and family. 
Being raised on the bayous of Louisiana, I grew up with Hurricane threats. Our family always stood back so I was slightly desensitized.  Back in 2003, Rickey and I decided to remain at our home with 3 small children for a CAT 1. I remember, shortly after losing power, the door blew open; I felt mesmerized by the power of wind. Like Helen Hunt in Twister, I ventured out the door to feel its force and could barely hold myself upright in the pitch blackness of the storm. It’s so different when you are responsible for others. As the house shook, I swore I would never stay back with my children again.  It felt so intense, yet I later found out it had lessened to only a Tropical Storm when it hit our home. 
Knowing I had safety from the elements right inside the house, I still get choked up when I think of that small moment, compared to what people experienced as their homes were swallowed by floods and were forced to endure the elements from their rooftops. A CAT 4 is unthinkable.
Prayers to everyone on the coast trying to find higher ground from Ida. If you can leave, physically, financially and without recourse, please remember that you are blessed with that ability.  Sadly enough, I know each of the cars seen heading out, left their homes, not knowing what they will come back to.  Some are just driving without a hotel reservation available. Gas pumps are out of service, businesses and fast-food places are boarded up. It’s scary and is why many decide to shelter in their homes. I pray you find safe shelter and peace over the next few days until you can return home. 
Prayers for those who take care of the elderly, sick, or special needs family members.  It’s hard for me to take Adam down the street, much less evacuate with him. I would need so much equipment, medicine, nourishment, oxygen, clean water, batteries, … for an extended period and if I’d stay, there would be no way for me to protect him without power. The hospitals are full of very sick people, so Covid adds a whole new layer of how to prepare. My heart goes out to all of you needing to make tough decisions. 
Everyone has unique situations so let us also please pray for those who remain in Ida’s path. Prayers to all the first responders, coast guard, rescue divers, nurses, doctors, EMS, nursing home facilities, prison staff, electrical company lineman, offshore oilmen, those running the power plants and generators, and the list goes on for those who must stay and shelter in place.
Prayers to the many small business owners and fisherman, including my dad and uncles, who stay during hurricanes. They respect mother nature and do not do so lightly. Please pray specifically for their safety from rising storm surge, tornadoes, flying debris, down power lines as they maintain control of their fleet and protect their livelihoods. 
In 2005, our town (and New Orleans) was on the left, safer side of Hurricane Katrina as it landed in Pass Christian, Mississippi as a Category 3.  Ida is expected to be a 4.  Dire warnings are now normalized with each strong storm.  Residents who stay, despite mandatory evacuation, are asked to write their name, address, and Social Security Number on their body parts for identification.  To protect the first responders, aid is not provided to any residents if they call for rescue. Our town has been under this order since yesterday. 
I feel completely helpless all the way in PA, knowing Jean Lafitte will lie in the most dangerous upper right quadrant as Ida makes landfall. Our town is 30 miles directly south of New Orleans with no hurricane protection levees.  The window to get out is closing as the roads will be barricaded at 11am tomorrow.  Storm surge is predicted at 11- 15 ft., with the most dangerous water and winds ripping through the area. 
This evening, law enforcement has started going door to door asking for a head count of those remaining inside homes.   Let that sink in for a moment.  It’s not our place to judge but we can pray.

I’m a crying mess thinking of your anxiety of the unknown. 
I love you fiercely.

God, please wrap your arms around my hometown, our special friends and family. May our bayou ancestors help protect you all once again. See less

=== Post 16 ===
Date: Sunday, June 20, 2021 at 3:36 PM
Shown as: June 20, 2021
Link: https://www.facebook.com/AdamsVillageofHope/posts/pfbid02oRmGvCUwsYCaAKsDiPBaNxEYT5rLdfD5nQvMKMmZnBPzwiQUo7GtyigXVN8kbAPPl

Through the years, I’ve been fortunate to send my kids on long summer breaks to spend time learning the ways of the water from my dad. My kids relate home with carefree time on the bayou and their grandparents. Recently, Lea has been talking alot about fishing and the stars and it made me think about my own childhood, as a kid around her age. 
My favorite memories with my dad are on his boat.  During the summer, I would beg to go trawling with him. Our three-day trip always started with a long walk from my home, through my grandparents’ yard, across the street, a stop at the fig tree for a snack, and there, tied to the wharf, I would find the boat and my dad’s welcoming smile. 
At the dock for fuel and ice, the workers would say “I see you brought your best deckhand today” and I would smile. On the ride out, I would climb the riggings while his boat was in full speed, sit in the special chair he welded at the top (just for me), stretch my arms out and soar. He showed me that beauty was not just in the spectacular Louisiana sunsets, but all around us if we looked for it. Late at night, he would get me out of the bed with his flashlight in hand to show me what the shrimp looked like as they jumped over the nets. He would explain the stars and how the tide was controlled by the moon.  When we would trawl on anchor, I would fall asleep to the gentle rocking of the boat, the quiet sounds of waves splashing and absolutely nothing else.

Rise Up: Becoming Stronger 
Around 4 or 5 months after Adam was born, I was probably the unhealthiest I had been my entire life.   Lack of sleep, cabin fever, 2 little ones, 3 big ones, in the midst of being a mom, I had totally lost myself. With baby weight, poor nutrition and a new hip injury, it didn't take long to lose all motivation.  I was severely overweight for my 5'1 frame. I refused to look at a scale, but my best guess was 180. Coming from a closet filled with size 2/4 clothing, I didn't even recognize myself any more. I knew I had to do something, so I started small. Out with the snacks, in with movement.  I didn't work out, but I made the most of incorporating exercise into my daily routine.  Adam nursed every 3 hours for the first 10 months of his life. It was only when he started whole food that he slept through the night and I started making any progress in self-care. I was beginning to feel myself again. Life was good.  We enjoyed a sense of 'controlled chaos' as a family of 7 and my body felt it. Down about 20lbs, I was finally seeing a sliver of light in what I 'thought' was darkness. Ha.
Then, on an unexpected sunny day, I lost control of everything in our world in a matter of minutes. For 8 months that followed Adam's drowning, I gave into all levels of convenience.  Sometimes, I ate junk all day for comfort.  Most days, I didn't feel worthy to eat at all. Survival was the only thing that mattered. Self-care was nowhere in the plan.
I read inspirational posts from my friend, Nicole- owner of Point of Fitness.  I convinced myself I would wait to join her gym until the time was right, when I was settled with our new normal. Then it was Thanksgiving, then Christmas. Even the thought of a New Year resolution was not enough. The list of easy excuses not to sign up to a gym were a mile long.  Last March, at a wellness visit, my BMI came back as 'obese'. The scale didn't lie. I certainly wasn't trying to be healthy and I immediately felt helpless. This would be just one more thing out of my control. 
In a minimally conscious state, Adam can no longer carry his weight.  It wasn't long before I realized the need to become physically fit just to lift him.  Surviving my sons drowning wasn’t enough, I had to become stronger than ever before. I cried for a week, struggled with excuses and then one night while watching Adam fight to stay healthy, I remembered- WHO I WAS and WHAT WE'VE OVERCOME. Adam has forced me, more than once, to realize what we take for granted and abuse daily.  How could I work so hard on keeping him healthy and not give myself, his caregiver, the same respect? It would have been easy to use our tragedy as an excuse forever, but I needed to be strong. I needed to be present. I needed to set the example for all of my children.  
"I'm in!"  
Never had I typed 2 words so fast and hit enter.  Nicole responded immediately, officially securing my spot in the April Downsize group and that was it. My perception of "WHY" changed and a healthier, more purposeful life style followed.
Downsize, by definition, is a verb that means to make (something) smaller and I can happily say that it has lived up to its meaning.  However, that smaller number on the scale will never overshadow the things I've gained in less than 3 months: self-confidence, self-worth, the ability to inspire others, to set a beautiful example of what it means to treat your body with respect, overcome cravings and most importantly, have the strength, physically and mentally, to push forward on this special journey. I feel a new level of peace and life energy in the way I breathe, when I grab something easily off the floor, or the way I smile after completing an exercise class.  I evaluate everything that I consume for portion size and its main purpose in my body.  It’s second nature now. When I decide to have some popcorn at the movies or a drink with friends, I don’t even feel guilty.  I make great choices all day to fuel my body so it will continue to support me even when I celebrate every now and then.

September Tears
Christmas, Birthdays, Easter...these are supposed to be hard. We prepare for these.  We avoid the toy section at all costs. We dodge the bike and sports areas. We build strong defenses... we know it’s coming, and we slowly let the sadness ease over us. Just when we get through with Memorial Day and June 5th, then the drowning season starts. Every day a new report of a family devastated because layers of protection were not in place. Honestly, I have yet to find a month that seems ok to breathe in. I think it's just being here at our home- passing the pool every day. Seeing him floating. Seeing Lea floating. It never ends. I can't count the times I fling the door open and run out to scan the bottom of the deep end because a sunken toy casts a shadow. Frankly, I wish I could hibernate right through the whole god damn warm season. 
With so much to do once school started, I thought little time would be left for feelings. But, no. September had a sadness all its own. Triggers were everywhere. Every event we did with our older children was a clear reminder of things Adam will never experience. Little Kindergarten desks and playground monkey bars with Lea, two a day soccer practices with Ave, driving on the interstate with Grace behind the wheel, moving my oldest son into his college dorm. These normal activities had me secretly a wreck for weeks.  It's so hard to share their happiness and not allow even a glimpse of sadness to shine through to dampen the moment. Believe me when I say that the guilt is ever present.  Guilt of the accident. Guilt were not ‘being enough’ for our other children.  Guilt that we can no longer go places easily or afford things we once could.  This is the reality we face now.  So, the pain stays hidden, just under the surface of our smiles, our polite conversation, and our laughter.  Some days it is so severe, we could win awards on our performances. Unfortunately, at times, our children could too. This is how it needs to be for us, for them and even for you. 
There is a never-ending cycle of feeling productive, with laser-focus goals, then having one toy, one outfit, one song, send me hurling back into the abyss of sadness.  When I allow myself to dwell, the darkness is all consuming. I feel this is common with grief. It steals your joy. It grabs you from behind the neck and slowly squeezes the life force out of you. It takes away your ability to breathe. Sometimes, you let it because you feel you deserve to sense this panic. You deserve to experience how he felt. Without outwardly crumbling to pieces, you realize you are doing a terrible job, the bare minimum, to get what everyone needs. And through the whole process, you must appear, to everyone around, that you are okay.  But who are you kidding? You’ve let dishes pile up. You haven’t been to the gym in 6 weeks. You have stacks of unpaid notices on table.  You haven’t cooked in a very long time. The weeds are out of control. You care about being presentable, but- you really don’t.  And then you relax, breathe life in and remain still. You surface, shake it off and the process starts again. “What more can I do?”  
Adam was the picture of health for the first 15 months of his life. For the last 16 months, he has been fighting every day to do things that we take for granted, to breathe, blink, swallow, and bend.  I want my happy, flirting trouble maker back, stealing hearts with his smile alone. Some days I'm torn between wanting to remember how he used to greet me in the morning with the biggest, cooing grin, bouncing up and down, holding the crib rail for support, trying to stick his fat thighs through the slants, and some days, I wish God would just wipe these memories away so I wouldn't feel such a painful loss. But the thought of never seeing his smile again leaves me gasping for air.  Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy, Cranial Electro Stimulation, Binary beats, Low Laser Light Therapy, Reiki, chiropractic care, CVI Therapy, Occupational Therapy, Physical Therapy, the list goes on and we try them all, for some hope of a connection. I'm so angry that this is our path. I want to be blissfully ignorant of the medically complex world we are in now. I want to sleep, not worried about every machine that goes beep in the night or what pain he may be experiencing when his heart rate soars. I beg him for a smile. His whole face trembles as he tries. My heart aches when I think he's been trapped inside a body that can't function easily or communicate with us for MORE THAN HALF his life. It’s just not fair. But above all… I'm terrified that he’s tired of trying.  So, with exhaustion, September tears fell once again.
After viewing videos of myself working with Adam, I became overwhelmed.  Why do I always push him for more? When will it be enough? It’s a fine line of how far we’ve come and how much work we still need to do… I fight daily with this internal struggle. And then, in a crowded restaurant over breakfast, Rickey reminded me of the effort it takes for Adam to communicate.  “Although his outward appearance cannot reflect emotion, just as his head cannot turn, it doesn’t mean that he’s not happy. Why does he need to prove it? Adam ‘smiles’ every time you walk in a room.” A sobbing mess, I finally heard that message. Rather than request a smile, I decided to appreciate his expression for what it is. I look deep into his eyes and see his beautiful face. Smile or no smile, I thank him for such hard work. I kiss his eyes, his nose, his lips, his fingers and his toes- I smile ‘for him’ because I can but I don't need to. I feel his smile inside.  One word, yet so complicated, powerful, and terrifying, all at once… Acceptance.
I know there are highs and lows in this madness we call life. I rely on the fact that I’m not the only person who feels this way. So, if you do, step back, away from the fake stuff in life that makes no difference, and really look at what is happening around you, not “to you”. Believe in miracles you can’t see and always know that you can surface, even from quicksand.  You just need to relax, breathe in and be still. You may have different circumstances that lead you to this desert but know that saving is from within.
October is finally here now. The pool is covered.  The season is changing and with it something new is happening. There is a sliver of light, a visual connection, more awareness.... could it be… a smile. 
I cautiously hold back the excitement. My heart believes it, but my brain has been tricked before by seizures.  Then, he does it again and the next day. And each time I breathe this moment in, I relive the joy I felt when he first grinned.  I did not request it. It was not given on demand.  Adam’s smile is on His time and that is what makes it such a beautiful sight to witness.  My heart is heavy for all the right reasons and we battle forward, stealing yours one smile at a time.

They made a Deal
Jesus reached down on June 5, 2017 and took Adams hand.  Adam laughed with Him and ran through the fields of Heaven. After 90 minutes, they came to a tree with an old swing and Adam rested on His lap.  
Out of breath Adam asked, "Are we ready to head back now?" Then Jesus said, "We could, or you could stay here with us?" as he pointed to a group just over the hill. Some were old, some were young- all were familiar.

It seemed the longer they played, the happier he was.  
On the other hand, he knew he would miss his family terribly.  Adam torn between the two asked, "Could I do both?" 
They made a deal.  
Part of Adam could stay and run and laugh with the village and part of Adam would be able to go back to his family for a short period.
During that time, he would be showered with love every day, but it wouldn't be easy. As a soldier of God, he would have lots of work to do. Being in two places at once would also be hard on him. Many of the things he loved to do, his Earthly body would need to relearn.
Adam was worried he wouldn't succeed in God's plan.  'Jesus', Adam pondered nervously, "what if I can't find my voice to tell them I'm okay?" 
Jesus smiled, "Adam, without saying a word, you will speak volumes. Without hearing, you will feel every Prayer spoken from those who love you. Though you may not walk, your footprints will make a path for others to follow.  Regardless the distance, you will touch complete strangers in ways that only I know.  I will never leave your side and when you feel your work is done, you may come Home again and be complete.
Although you may leave pieces of yourself here in Heaven, those who love you on Earth will never see you as anything but perfect." 
Adam still a little concerned softly whispered, “But, when I’m here in Heaven, won’t I miss mom and dad, my brother and sisters, and my best friend Lea?” 
God gave him a big bear hug, “Adam I knew you would feel this way. But you should know that when you took my hand, you didn’t come alone. Without knowing, you took a small part of your family with you.

beep...."We have a pulse"
This story helps me make sense of a senseless world.  Why some die, why others come back but are never the same.

After being gone for 90 minutes, I feel Adam made the decision to come back- for us.  In doing so, he chose his heavenly body to keep his ability to run infinitely, to giggle endlessly, and to hold some of his happiest memories of our family.

Brother, watch this!” as she did a cannonball and swam 40 feet to the other side of the pool, floating on her back when needing to regroup. WHY did we not have a fence separating the house from the pool.  WHY did I worried more about the view and not fully concern myself about that layer of safety? WHY did I rely on door alarms that stop signaling when the door is mostly closed but not completely shut? WHY did I feel my children were above drowning? WHY us? WHY him!!!  I hate the pool and as soon as I muttered those words, another one caught my attention. As I looked across the field, not just a few but, thousands of fireflies were in sync, lighting Adam’s tree and our field. Our village shines just as bright as the fireflies’ village. And in the most spectacular fashion, I am reminded again that we will never need to walk this journey alone. In the faces of everyone in our village, “God, I see you.”

By Kate Ousley 
I think there is a great misconception about tragedy--that you will someday be healed. Even the thought that you MUST be healed. That someday the pain will be gone. You must get over it. You will not.

"OR MORE, OR LESS"
Lea turns 5 this week and a couple days later, Grace turns 16.  Thinking about birthdays reminded me of a post I started back in November.  Figured I’d finish it…
So, I turned 40 last month and was supposed to be in Disney with my family that week. The older girls said they would opt out, so a quick trip with just our little ones was the plan. It would be Adams first visit.  He would get his first haircut on Main Street, followed by his first set of ears. We would spend all our time at kiddie land or sitting on a parade route. A perfect no rush kind of weekend.  My day spent eating beautiful deserts from Epcot while my Lil ones would enjoy 'trying' to eat mickey ice cream bars before they would melt in the fall weather.  I planned all spring; this was my 'Big 40th Birthday Wish’!
But instead, on Nov 8th, I woke in PA with my lil man attached to a ventilator.  Ironically, he was wearing a Buzz Lightyear sweatshirt. I didn't even think about Disney the day before when I put it on him, so I sort of laughed when I made the connection.  Small consolation to a trip but when I mentioned his Buzz shirt, Adam's eyes lit up and moved all around.   We’re not sure if the area in his brain that holds long and short-term memories was damaged in the accident. His past is either gone or tucked away inside for only him to enjoy for now.  So… could he really remember who Buzz Lightyear was? 
Once again, hope filled my heart. HOPE- that was HIS never-ending gift to me.   I sang Happy Birthday to myself (from him) and kissed his sweet cheeks for the lovely present. 
It turned out to be a beautiful sunny day. With Epcot still on my mind, we went out for a Mexican lunch and it was fantastic. It was a normal day of work, household chores and kids’ stuff: projects due, carpool, dinner, and bath time. Life sluggishly still goes on. 
We cut a cake on Sunday and I requested we not do it again. I just felt terrible eating cake in front of Adam. He loved cake! The last cake at our house was celebrating Joyce's birthday in May. In the video, Adam patiently does sign language signaling with his little hands "more... more…" while waiting for his piece. He was so smart. That's all I could think about the entire time they sang for me and Adam watched from the sidelines.  He was supposed to be sitting on my lap trying to stick his finger in the icing.  Could he possibly have remembered that too? 
I know people often dread getting older… that was never me.  But now, I have a new fear of aging and it's not about gray hair or wrinkles. With every passing birthday, I worry about not being well enough, healthy enough to accomplish what I do for Adam now.  Having children late in life has been a wonderful blessing.  We certainly have more patience and a better understanding of priorities than when I was 21 and pregnant with my first or 25 with 3 ankle-biters under 4.  But at least with my older ones... energy was never a factor and the thought of not being around for them or my grandchildren was a foreign idea.  No one wants to think this way, but raising a child with special needs is an entirely different kind of stress.  Who would look after him once we were gone? 
The first days we were back home, I remember one of girls needing to go somewhere and of course, I couldn't bring her. She said in a salty tone "IS THAT IT?  YOU CAN'T DO ANYTHING EVER AGAIN.  YOU JUST NEED TO SIT IN THIS ROOM AND TAKE CARE OF ADAM FOR THE NEXT 18 YEARS."  
I was sad and a bit shocked. Not by her anger, she had every right to be angry.  I was shocked by her timeline.  Knowing that normally at 18, Adam would leave the house, go to college, and make his way in the world. But that was before.  I responded, quietly... "Or more" so she understood the reality of his lifelong disability.  Then, with a rattle in my voice I whispered... "Or less" which was much more heartbreaking to say. Her face immediately showed she understood the gravity of those two words.  She turned away and left. It was never brought up again. 
We knew making the decision of 'hope' at the hospital was one we were making for our children as well. That alone was why it was such a tough, gut wrenching time.  They would eventually be the decision makers for Adam. They would eventually bear the burden and the heartache of his day to day care. We were drafting them into an army they couldn't fully sign up for and could never turn away from. 
When Adam turns 18, I'll be 57 and my husband will be 60. My older son will be 36, and my girls will be 33, 32 and 22.  I need time to freeze while science catches up. The reality is time does not stop. It frightens me to think that I can barely carry him across the house now.  How will I manage in a couple of years?  What will Adam look like at 18, or more?
The fact is I can't think about the "or more" and I certainly cannot bring myself to comprehend the "or less".  I can only think about today. 
No human has control over how much time we have left, but today I can get out of bed. Today, I can put one foot in front of the other.  Today, I can rejoice in the fact that Adam is still with us where I can physically pick him up, smell his freshly washed skin and pass my fingers through his curls.  Today, I can tell him I love him and smother him in kisses, and even though he can’t respond, I can feel the love in his breathing, his tear-filled eyes and in his relaxed comfort when I cradle him.  
Today, I’ll use my Birthday Wish for you. 
I wish that you can experience the raw emotions as the parent of a special needs child without ever being labeled one.  SACRIFICE, HELPLESSNESS, SADNESS, and EXHAUSTION take on a new meaning when you are set on a journey like ours but so do the rewards of PATIENCE, HOPE, EMPATHY, UNCONDITIONAL LOVE, and FAITH.
It shouldn't take sickness to make you realize that you must live life for today. You must reach out to those you are fighting with and offer grace. How many of you have not spoken to a family member over something silly?  Make TODAY, the day YOU change that. Don’t wait for them to apologize.  Send flowers to someone while they are alive rather to a funeral home once it’s too late.  Create traditions with those you love so you can be remembered. Buy the birthday cake and CELEBRATE the added candle. Tomorrow is never promised. You are here today. You have come this far. God will find a way for everything else to work out.
As for our family, Lea will get to show Adam the mouse one day.  We will get a haircut on Main Street and I’ll push him around wearing our ears proudly.  I may not have been in Disney last November, but being with my family made me realize once again, I was right where I was supposed to be for my birthday... 
The ‘Happiest Place on Earth’ is wherever my loved ones are, and it always will be.      
And if I’m really lucky, we’ll get to celebrate Adam’s 18th, his 21st, and even his 40th birthday ‘together’ on Main Street… and maybe just maybe he’ll be the one pushing me.

There were no smiles. No peak-a-boos through the crib bars. No sweet sounds of childhood. How I even wished that he would cry, but he didn’t.  The tough part is that somewhere in my mind, I felt that Adam would be better once we returned. First days of school, new boyfriends, new teachers, entire sports seasons that were gone.  I soaked in every word they would dish out and I sat amazed at how mature they had become. But instead, they moved through the conversation as if nothing had changed.  They have been through so much and yet from the very beginning, they worried about shielding me. While in the water, the rising and falling of the waves were almost memorizing and somewhere in the calmness I lost track. In a panic, my eyes rushed to find them. AJ is settling in for a fight!  The MRI they did last night did not image his brain again, we misunderstood the doctor.  He said that it was too early to re-image.  Instead, it focused on his spinal column and the results were negative (no spinal injury). The nurse said he arched his back a little when the took his temp and is continuing to react to touch.  His reactions are becoming somewhat more pronounced (he moved his leg today).  They reduced the ventilator again, removed his catheter and his C-collar, took him off the antibiotics, reduced his pain meds and increased his feeding.  The doctor said all of his organs look great and can function on their own IF his brain is able to relay the information.  The elephant in the room is the severity of the brain injury.  They still agree it's significant BUT are now wanting to give him a few days to see how he continues to progress.  Another EEG is scheduled for tomorrow and we are looking forward to washing all of that glue out of his hair again! See less
