# Research notes & sources

Where every statistic on our site comes from, and which popular "facts" we avoid. The numbers on the site are edited in the Studio (**Statistics**); this file explains the sources behind them.

> **Gathered in September 2026.** Before launch, we should click each source link and make sure the wording still matches. Items marked **[verify]** had conflicting wording in different places. We'll re-check everything every spring before National Water Safety Month, when CDC and CPSC usually release new numbers.

## 1. Headline statistics

- **Ages 1–4:** "More children ages 1–4 die from drowning than any other cause of death." (CDC now describes this as the leading cause of death from *any* cause for this age group.) [CDC Drowning Facts](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/data-research/facts/index.html)
- **Ages 5–14:** second leading cause of unintentional-injury death, after motor vehicle crashes. (CDC, same page)
- **Deaths per year (U.S., all ages):** average **4,083**/yr in 2012–2021 (CDC facts page). **More than 4,500**/yr in 2020–2022, about 500 more per year than 2019 ([CDC Vital Signs, May 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/vitalsigns/drowning/index.html)).
- **Rates:** 1.2 per 100,000 in 2019 → 1.4 (2020), 1.4 (2021), 1.3 (2022). Ages 1–4: **3.1** per 100,000 in 2021 and 2022, up ~28% from 2019. ([MMWR 73(20)](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7320e1.htm))
- **Children & teens (<20):** 981 died from drowning in 2023. ([AAP Policy Statement, *Pediatrics*, July 2026](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/158/1/e2026077410/207630/Prevention-of-Drowning-Policy-Statement))
- **Nonfatal ED visits:** average **8,111**/yr (2012–2021). (CDC facts page)
- **Ratio:** "For every child under age 18 who dies from drowning, another **7** receive emergency department care for nonfatal drowning." (CDC facts page)

### CPSC Pool or Spa Submersion report (2026 edition)
[CPSC news release, June 2026](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2026/CPSC-Report-Highlights-Persistent-Risk-of-Childhood-Drowning)
- Average **376** children under 15 died per year in pool/spa drownings (2021–2023); **nearly 80% were younger than 5**.
- 379 died in 2023, up 6% from 359 in 2022.
- Estimated **5,900** ED-treated nonfatal pool/spa drowning injuries per year (2023–2025).
- **More than 70%** of the deaths happened at **residential** locations (the child's home, or a family member's, friend's or neighbor's).
- Deaths peak in **June, July and August**.
- [verify] The share of *nonfatal* injuries among children under 5 in the 2026 report. The last confirmed figure is **73% (2022–2024)** in the [2025 report (PDF)](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/Pool-or-Spa-Submersion-Estimated-Nonfatal-Drowning-Injuries-and-Reported-Drownings-2025-Report.pdf).

### Where young children drown
- Ages 1–4: most often in **home swimming pools**. Infants under 1: most often in **bathtubs**. ([CDC risk factors](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/risk-factors/index.html); [NCHS Data Brief 413](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db413.htm)) [verify the exact fraction for bathtubs: indexed text shows both "two-thirds" and "three quarters"]

## 2. Nonfatal drowning

- **Definition:** CDC: a person survives a drowning, with outcomes ranging "from no injuries to very serious injuries such as brain damage or permanent disability." WHO: drowning is "the process of experiencing respiratory impairment from submersion/immersion in liquid."
- **Hospitalization:** nearly **40%** of drownings treated in EDs need hospitalization or transfer, vs. ~10% for all unintentional injuries. (CDC facts page)
- **Outcomes:** severe brain damage can cause memory problems, learning disabilities, or permanent loss of basic functioning. (CDC)
- **What predicts outcome:** duration of submersion is the most consistent predictor. Under 5 minutes is associated with favorable outcomes; over 25 minutes with very poor outcomes. ([Quan et al., *Resuscitation*, 2016](https://www.resuscitationjournal.com/article/S0300-9572(16)30019-3/abstract)) Long-term outcome depends mainly on the severity of the hypoxic-ischemic brain injury and how fast effective resuscitation starts. ([Suominen & Vähätalo, 2012](https://sjtrem.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1757-7241-20-55))
- **Cost to society:** drowning injuries and deaths cost the U.S. **$53 billion** in 2020. ([CDC at-a-glance PDF](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/pdf/CDC-DIP_At-a-Glance_Drowning_508.pdf))
- ❌ No credible source found for a per-child "lifetime cost of care" (e.g. the often-quoted "$4.5 million"). Don't use it.

## 3. How fast and quiet

- CDC: drowning "happens in seconds and is often silent."
- AAP (HealthyChildren.org): young children "can drown in as little as an inch or two of water… quickly and silently." ([link](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Water-Safety-And-Young-Children.aspx))
- American Red Cross (May 2026): drowning "can happen in less than a minute, even in just a few inches of water." ([link](https://www.redcross.org/about-us/news-and-events/press-release/2026/national-water-safety-month--drowning-is-preventable-.html))
- Szpilman et al., *NEJM* 2012: from submersion to cardiac arrest usually takes "seconds to a few minutes." ([link](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra1013317))

## 4. Layers of protection: the evidence

- **Principle:** AAP (2026): multiple layers are needed "because no single method is effective." Proven strategies: 4-sided isolation fencing with self-closing, self-latching gates; close, constant, attentive supervision; swimming competency; life jackets when boating; early rescue and resuscitation.
- **Supervision:** AAP recommends an adult who can swim, within arm's length, giving **"touch supervision"** to infants, toddlers and weak swimmers. ([AAP news](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/AAP-releases-updated-drowning-prevention-recommendations.aspx)) **Water Watcher:** one designated adult, no distractions, handing off (often every 15 minutes). (NDPA, Safe Kids)
- **Fencing:** Cochrane review (Thompson & Rivara, 1998, 3 case-control studies): isolation (4-sided) vs. 3-sided fencing, **odds ratio 0.17** (95% CI 0.07–0.44). That's the source of the widely cited "83% reduction." The evidence is old but consistent. ([Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD001047_fencing-which-completely-encloses-all-sides-swimming-pool-and-isolates-it-home-effective-preventing))
  - AAP fence specs: at least **4 ft** high, gate opens **away** from the pool, latch at least **54 in** high, gaps no wider than **4 in**.
  - CDC modeling (*Injury Prevention*, 2025): isolation fencing + life jackets could prevent ~348 deaths and $4.5 billion in costs per year. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12353649/))
- **Door alarms:** where the house forms part of the pool barrier, CPSC guidelines and building codes call for door alarms: listed to **UL 2017**, sounding within **7 seconds** of the door opening, for at least **30 seconds**, pausable for no more than **15 seconds**. AAP also recommends door locks/alarms or a security system door chime. ([CPSC Safety Barrier Guidelines, PDF](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/pdfs/blk_media_SafetyBarrierGuidelinesResPools.pdf))
- **Covers:** safety covers should meet **ASTM F1346**. Code accepts a powered F1346 cover in place of door alarms.
- **Drains:** the Virginia Graeme Baker Pool & Spa Safety Act (effective Dec 19, 2008) requires anti-entrapment drain covers in public pools and spas. ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/content/Virginia-Graeme-Baker-Pool-and-Spa-Safety-Act-0))
- **Swim lessons:** AAP supports lessons **after age 1**. Brenner et al. 2009 found formal lessons were associated with an **88% lower** drowning risk at ages 1–4, *but* the estimate is very imprecise (adjusted OR 0.12, 95% CI 0.01–0.97). No lesson type has been proven superior, programs for infants under 1 are unproven, and lessons never "drown-proof" a child. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19255386/))
- **CPR:** 2024 AHA/AAP update: after drowning, **CPR with rescue breaths** is essential; compressions-only is for rescuers who are untrained or unwilling. ([AHA](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/updated-guidance-reaffirms-cpr-with-breaths-essential-for-cardiac-arrest-following-drowning))
- **Missing child:** check the pool or spa first, including neighbors' pools. ([PA Department of Health](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/health/business-registration-and-regulation/bathing-places/pool-safety))

## 5. High-risk moments

- **Slipping away** (CPSC study of children under 5 in AZ, CA, FL, late-1980s data, still reproduced in current CPSC barrier guidelines): **46%** last seen in the house; **69%** not expected to be at or in the pool; **77%** missing **5 minutes or less**; 65% at the family's own pool, 33% at relatives' or friends' pools. *Always cite as a 1980s study.*
- **No permission to be in the water:** in a study of U.S. pool/spa incidents (2000–2017), 86% of children had no permission to be in the water and 80% were alone. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8392632/)) [verify authors/journal before using]
- **Parties/gatherings:** no primary national statistic found. Use the Water Watcher message rather than a number. (Safe Kids' 2004 "Clear Danger" report found 88% of child drownings happened with at least one adult present. It's dated, so cite the year if we use it.)
- **Bathtubs:** AAP: "Most child drownings inside the home occur in bathtubs, usually during a lapse in adult supervision." ([HealthyChildren.org](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Water-Safety-And-Young-Children.aspx))
- **Small amounts of water:** the same AAP page says to empty "buckets and pails used for cleaning or painting… coolers with melted ice" and to "install latches or locks on toilet seat lids."
- **Cold water and clothes:** PA Fish & Boat: in water under 70°F, "cold water shock causes an involuntary gasp (often resulting in aspiration of water), hyperventilation, breathlessness and a reduced ability to control breathing and swim." ([link](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/fishandboat/boating/regulations/cold-weather-life-jacket))
- **Year-round:** we didn't find a solid national month-by-month number for young children, so the site doesn't use one. It says pool drownings *peak* in summer (CPSC) and that bathtubs, buckets, hot tubs and ponds are there all year.
- **Nighttime, sleepwalking and wandering:** no statistic used. The site describes it without numbers, plus the autism figure below.
- **Toddlers 12–36 months** are at highest risk. ([AAP 2021](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/148/2/e2021052227/179784/Prevention-of-Drowning))
- **Autism:** children with autism are about **160×** as likely to die from drowning as the general pediatric population. ([Guan & Li, *AJPH*, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28590851/)) AAP 2026 recommends adaptive swim lessons.

## 6. Disparities
([CDC Health Equity](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/health-equity/index.html), 1999–2019 data unless noted)
- Black people under 30 drown at **1.5×** the rate of White people; ages 5–9: **2.6×**; ages 10–14: **3.6×**.
- In **swimming pools**, Black children 10–14 drown at **7.6×** the rate of White children.
- American Indian/Alaska Native people under 30: **2×** (2.7× in natural water).
- CDC 2024: 37% of Black adults report they can't swim (vs. 15% overall); 63% have never had a swim lesson (vs. 55%).

## 7. Pennsylvania
- **Death rate:** about 0.76 per 100,000 in PA vs. 1.23 nationally (2015–2019), from a secondary source ([PA Capital-Star](https://penncapital-star.com/health-care/risk-of-drowning-more-prevalent-in-the-summer-months-the-numbers-racket/)). [verify via [CDC WISQARS](https://wisqars.cdc.gov/)]
- **PA Child Death Review:** pools, hot tubs and spas were the most common location of child drowning deaths (2014 report). ([PDF](https://www.ncfrp.org/wp-content/uploads/State-Docs/PA_CDR2014.pdf)) The 2025 report covers 2023 deaths. Look for its drowning numbers. ([PDF](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/health/documents/topics/documents/programs/2025%20CDR%20Annual%20Report.pdf))
- **Pool barrier law:** [34 Pa. Code § 403.26](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=%2Fsecure%2Fpacode%2Fdata%2F034%2Fchapter403%2Fs403.26.html): residential pools/spas must follow the **2021 ISPSC** (effective Jan 1, 2026). Barrier at least 48 in; gates self-closing, self-latching, opening outward. **But the code still allows the house wall to be one side of the barrier** if doors (and low windows) to the pool have alarms or other approved protection. That's weaker than AAP's four-sided isolation fencing, and a natural advocacy issue for this family.
- **Boating:** Nov 1–Apr 30, life jackets are required on boats under 16 ft and on all canoes, kayaks and paddleboards; ~80% of PA boating deaths involve people without life jackets. ([PA Fish & Boat](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/fishandboat/boating/regulations/cold-weather-life-jacket))

## 8. Awareness calendar
- **May:** National Water Safety Month ([nwsm.phta.org](https://nwsm.phta.org/))
- **Late June:** World's Largest Swimming Lesson ([wlsl.org](https://wlsl.org/))
- **July 25:** World Drowning Prevention Day (UN resolution A/RES/75/273) ([WHO](https://www.who.int/campaigns/world-drowning-prevention-day/2026))
- ⚠️ "Drowning Impact Awareness Month" (August, purple ribbons) is an **Arizona** observance, not a national one.
- **Global:** ~300,000 drowning deaths worldwide in 2021, a quarter of them children under 5. ([WHO, Dec 2024](https://www.who.int/news/item/13-12-2024-drowning-deaths-decline-globally-but-the-most-vulnerable-remain-at-risk))

## 9. ❌ Statistics to avoid (outdated or unsupported)

| Don't use | Why / use instead |
|---|---|
| "#2 cause of death for ages 1–14" | Old wording. Now **#1 for ages 1–4**, #2 unintentional-injury cause for 5–14 |
| "About 3,500–3,900 deaths a year" / "10 a day" | Pre-2020. Use ~4,000 (2012–2021 avg) or 4,500+ (2020–2022) |
| "5 ER visits for every death" / "more than 50% hospitalized" | Old CDC wording. Now **7** and **nearly 40%** |
| "Black children 5–19 are 5.5× more likely to drown in pools" | 1999–2010 data. Now **7.6×** for ages 10–14 |
| "75% of child pool deaths are under 5" | CPSC now says **nearly 80%** |
| "88% happen with an adult present" | From 2004. Cite the year if used |
| "Most drown within 25 yards of a parent" | No source found |
| "70% of toddler drownings happen during non-swim time" | Traces to the 1980s CPSC study. Cite it that way (69%) |
| "2 minutes to unconsciousness, 4–6 minutes to brain damage" / "a child drowns in 20 seconds" | Poorly sourced. The "20–60 seconds" figure describes lifeguard observations of struggling swimmers, not toddlers who slip under silently |
| "Dry drowning" / "secondary drowning" | Not medical terms ([Red Cross explainer](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/articles/dry-or-delayed-secondary-drowning)) |
| "$4.5 million lifetime cost of care" | No credible source |
| "Swim lessons cut drowning 88%" stated as fact | Only with the imprecision caveat above |

**Patterns worth copying:** founder story + one memorable line · a physical **Water Watcher tag** · a pledge · navigation by audience · free printables · one signature annual event · merch that teaches.

**Gaps Because of ADAM can fill:** a **survivor-family voice** (nearly every peer is a memorial organization) · the **"alarms were off that one day"** lesson, which shows why layers matter · a dad who teaches infant swim · a **Central Pennsylvania** home base, where no big drowning prevention organization is based · an interactive "how fast / how layered" experience, which we didn't see on peer sites.
