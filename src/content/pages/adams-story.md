## June 5, 2017

It was supposed to be one of the happiest days of the year. Our oldest son was graduating from high school, our daughter had her first softball game, and the house in Duncansville, Pennsylvania was full of family who had come to celebrate.

Adam was 14 months old. With people coming and going and everyone getting ready to head out the door, he slipped outside. About three minutes later he was found in our backyard pool, face down.

## The fight for his life

His parents and grandparents started CPR right away. First responders took over, and then the emergency team at the local hospital. They worked on Adam for a very long time. Our family believes his heart started beating again after he was baptized in the emergency room. He was flown to UPMC Children’s Hospital of Pittsburgh.

Doctors prepared us for the worst. Adam had other plans.

## Life after a nonfatal drowning

Adam survived. But surviving a drowning isn’t the end of the story. Very often it’s the beginning of a new one.

When Adam came home to Blair County, he started early intervention physical and occupational therapy. We traveled to Louisiana for hyperbaric oxygen therapy, which insurance didn’t cover. Adam has defied his doctors’ predictions again and again, and he still lives with the effects of that day.

## Why we’re speaking up

> It only takes a second.

We had alarms on our doors. That day, with a house full of people coming and going, they were switched off. We share that not to blame anyone, but because it’s exactly how drowning happens to loving, careful families: on a busy day, in a moment nobody expected, in total silence.

Because of Adam, our family started teaching others. Rickey teaches infant swim lessons. We talk to anyone who will listen about pool fences, alarms, Water Watchers and CPR. We started **Because of ADAM: A Drowning Awareness Movement** so that no other family has to learn these lessons the way we did.

## Adam’s village

Thousands of people have followed Adam’s journey through our Facebook page, **Adam’s Village of Hope**. They prayed for him, cheered for every milestone, and showed up at fundraisers. That village is the heart of this movement.
