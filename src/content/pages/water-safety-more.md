## Teens and older kids

{{TODO: family review}}

Big kids drown too, and it often looks different than it does for toddlers. The American Academy of Pediatrics says:

- **Teens are more likely to overestimate their skills** and underestimate dangerous situations.
- **Teens are more likely to drown in lakes, rivers, ponds and other natural water** than in pools.
- **Alcohol is a major cause** in up to 70% of deaths linked to water recreation among U.S. teens.
- **Swim where there are lifeguards, and never swim alone.** Use the buddy system.
- **Knowing how to swim doesn’t make anyone drown-proof,** at any age.

Source: [American Academy of Pediatrics, Water Safety for Older Children](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Water-Safety-for-Older-Children.aspx)

## Life jackets

{{TODO: family review}}

- **Look for U.S. Coast Guard approval** on the label.
- **Fit it by weight.** Check the label for the weight range (and chest size for adults), then try it on your child ([U.S. Coast Guard](https://www.uscgboating.org/recreational-boaters/life-jacket-wear-wearing-your-life-jacket.php); [Pennsylvania Fish & Boat Commission](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/fishandboat/boating/basics/equipment/life-jackets)).
- **Floaties aren’t life jackets.** Water wings, arm bands and other “floaties” don’t prevent drowning and can slip off. A Coast Guard-approved life jacket protects better ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/summer-swim-safety.html)). Pool floats, inflatable toys and rafts aren’t life jackets either ([Pennsylvania Fish & Boat Commission](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/fishandboat/boating/basics/equipment/life-jackets)).
- **No vest is a babysitter.** Even a Coast Guard-approved life jacket or swim vest doesn’t replace an adult watching. As the Pennsylvania Fish & Boat Commission puts it, life jackets are not babysitters.
- **Pennsylvania law:** children 12 and under must wear one on boats 20 feet or shorter while moving, and in all canoes, kayaks and paddleboards. See [Water Safety in Pennsylvania](/pennsylvania).

## Check your above-ground pool for a recall

{{TODO: family review}}

Have an above-ground pool 48 inches or taller? About 5 million Bestway, Intex and Polygroup pools were recalled in July 2025 because the compression strap around the outside can give a child a foothold to climb in. Nine young children died. [Check your pool on the CPSC recall page](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2025/Bestway-Intex-and-Polygroup-Recall-Certain-Above-Ground-Pools-48-Inches-and-Taller-Due-to-Drowning-Hazard-Nine-Deaths-Reported-Five-Million-Pools-Sold-Since-2002) and see what to do.

## Learn CPR

{{TODO: family review}}

**In an emergency, call 911.** Then start CPR if you’re trained.

Our mom, Shannon, started CPR on Adam. Pops (our grandfather), our dad and the first responders all did CPR too. Take a class that includes infant and child CPR, so you’re ready too:

- [American Red Cross: find a CPR class](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/cpr)
- [American Heart Association: find a class](https://atlas.heart.org/)

Near us, the [Southern Alleghenies EMS Council](https://saems.com/courses/) in Altoona is an American Heart Association training center, and some of its classes are open to the public. Call them at 814-201-2265 and ask whether an upcoming class includes infant and child CPR.
