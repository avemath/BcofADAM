## Why this matters to us

When Adam was about 6 months old, we looked for ISR lessons. The closest instructor was two hours away. So we told ourselves our locked doors and door alarms would be enough.

They weren’t. Adam was 14 months old when he slipped out of the house and into our pool, and he didn’t know how to roll onto his back and float. Mom has said that if ISR had been available nearby, she believes she might have found him floating on his back, waiting to be rescued.

After Adam drowned, Dad decided to be the change we wanted to see. He trained for weeks in Florida, became a certified ISR instructor, and started **Little Explorer Swim ISR** so families in our area wouldn’t have to drive two hours for these lessons.

> Skills before thrills. No matter the cost, the time commitment or the distance, sometimes there are no do-overs.
>
> *— Mom*

## What ISR is

**Infant Swimming Resource (ISR)** has taught self-rescue swimming to babies and young children since 1966. The idea is simple: if a child ends up in the water alone, they should know what to do to survive until an adult gets to them.

This isn’t “mommy and me” splash time. It’s survival skills, taught one child at a time by a certified instructor.

- **Babies about 6–12 months** learn to **roll onto their back and float**, relax, and breathe, and to stay floating until help comes.
- **Kids about 1–6 years** learn to **swim, roll onto their back to float and rest, then roll over and swim again** until they reach the side or the steps.

## How lessons work

- **One-on-one** with a certified ISR instructor, never in a group.
- **About 10 minutes a day, 5 days a week,** for several weeks. Short, daily lessons are how little bodies learn the skills.
- **Safety checks every day.** Parents share their child’s health history before lessons start and keep the instructor updated on things like sleep, eating and diapers along the way.
- **Practice in clothes.** Most kids who fall in aren’t wearing a swimsuit, so kids practice their skills in clothes and shoes near the end of lessons.
- **Refreshers** as kids grow, so their skills keep up with their bigger bodies.

## One layer, not the only layer

Swim skills are one layer of protection. They never replace the others. The American Academy of Pediatrics supports swim lessons for most kids starting around age 1, and says no child is ever “drown-proof.” An adult watching (a Water Watcher), a four-sided pool fence, door and pool alarms, and CPR still matter every single time.

We just want every child to have one more layer than Adam had.
